## Why this exists

`safe-revenue` asked for the GA business case to be refined into a concrete validation slice: target user,
first-user channel, pilot ask, willingness-to-pay hypothesis and a measurable decision threshold with a named
owner. This is that slice, and it is deliberately small enough to run in weeks rather than quarters.

## The slice

> **A developer who already writes code-generation templates — Razor, T4, Scriban, or a home-grown
> string-concatenation script — installs the CLI, and sees one of their own templates render in under five
> minutes, then chooses whether to pay for the thing that saves them time afterwards.**

Everything below serves that sentence.

## Target user

Not "developers". The narrowest group that feels this product's pain today:

**A .NET developer maintaining generated code by hand** — DTOs, clients, migrations — who has already
reached for a template engine, which means they have already decided the problem is real. They are reachable
in places where that decision is visible: a T4/Razor question, a source-generator issue, a "how do you keep
your DTOs in sync" thread.

Excluded on purpose: people who have never generated code (they need the problem explained before the
product), and teams with a codegen platform already (they are an enterprise sale, not a first-user pilot).

## First-user channel

Ranked by cost per qualified visitor — a *qualified* visitor has already decided the problem is real:

| # | Channel | Why it fits | Cost | Measure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The CLI itself, published** | the fastest path from interest to "it worked": one command, one template, one result | 2 pts of packaging work | installs, first successful render |
| 2 | **A single technical post** on what a template engine with a language server actually gives you | reaches the person already looking; no audience needed if it lands in a community that has the question | 1 pt | reads → installs |
| 3 | **Direct outreach to 20 named developers** with a repo using T4 or Razor codegen | highest signal per person; slowest per hundred | 2 pts | replies, pilots started |
| 4 | The site lane's landing page and plan catalogue | it exists already | 0 in this lane | signups |

**Start at 1 and 2 together**, because they are the only two that can be live within a week and they
compose: the post is useless without something to install, and the CLI is invisible without a place to hear
about it.

## The pilot ask

Not a demo, not a survey. **One template, one session:**

> "Give me one template you maintain by hand. I will render it with TinyTools in front of you, and you keep
> the result. Ten minutes. If it does not save you time, say so and I will go away."

That ask is chosen because it produces a **behaviour**, not an opinion. Someone who says "interesting" has
told you nothing; someone who hands over a real template has revealed that the pain is real and specific.

## Willingness-to-pay hypothesis

**Hypothesis:** a developer who has rendered one of their own templates will pay for one of two things —
**not for the CLI** (developers expect CLIs free and there is no precedent for charging for one), but for
either:

- **the hosted collaboration surface**: templates and their rendered output shared with a team, with history
  — the thing that cannot be done in a shell; or
- **the editor experience**: the VSIX, once it actually verifies in an editor. Tooling that saves a keystroke
  budget is priced like an IDE add-on, not like a library.

**Price point to test:** two tiers against the site lane's existing `PlanCatalogue` — a free single-user tier
that is genuinely useful (so the CLI spreads), and one paid team tier. Do not invent a price; the catalogue
already expresses the tiers, and this slice tests whether a *subject* can be recognised as entitled, which
is the actual missing link measured in `SPEC3341`.

**The hypothesis I would put real money against being wrong:** that people will pay for the *library*. Open
source template engines are free and good. The product's paid value is collaboration and the editor, and if
that is not true, the commercial gate is not worth building.

## Measurable decision threshold

Fixed **before** the pilot runs, because a threshold chosen afterwards is a rationalisation. Each threshold
now carries its **measurement source**, because a threshold without one is a wish — and the first version of
this table did not say where any number would come from.

| # | Metric | Threshold | Measured by | What it decides |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | Time from install to first successful render | **< 5 minutes**, median | **manual ledger**, consented start/stop per participant — no install event exists | the product is usable enough to spread |
| T2 | Qualified visitors → installs | **≥ 10%** - hypothesis, no measurement source today - Site confirms it has no per-product visitor or purchase measure and that checkout is disabled; this lane has no install source either | the channel reaches the right people |
| T3 | Installs → a pilot session with a real template | **≥ 30%** | **manual ledger** with the participant's own-template attestation | the ask works |
| T4 | Pilot sessions → a second template unprompted | **≥ 40%** | **manual observation** by the pilot runner — not countable from events | the pain is recurring, not one-off |
| T5 | Pilot sessions → willingness to pay at the catalogue price | **≥ 2 of 10** - hypothesis, no measurement source today - a consented interview against the Site's offer, but no price ask is authorised and participant access is not approved | a commercial gate is worth building now |
| T6 | Support burden per pilot | **< 30 minutes** | **local support-time log** per session | the product is not carried by hand-holding |

### Correction, 2026-11-28: this pilot does not need telemetry

`safe-revenue` mapped all six thresholds against the proposed event vocabulary in `SREV0007` and found that
**the events support almost none of them**. They are right, and the error was mine in two places:

1. **I claimed five of six thresholds were "event-shaped".** They are not. T1 needs an install timestamp that
   does not exist, T2 needs denominators owned by another lane, T3 needs an attestation no event can carry,
   T4 needs the *same person* (a boolean is not an identity key), T5 asks what a person *said* rather than
   what they did, and T6 is support time, not use time.
2. **I made instrumentation a prerequisite for the slice.** That was wrong, and it is the more damaging
   mistake because it blocked the cheapest, most informative experiment behind the most expensive one.

A pilot of ten people is measured by **a human with a ledger**. Telemetry earns its place at scale, when the
cohort is too large to witness — not here. So this slice is **not blocked by `SPEC3343`**; it can run with
nothing more than consent and a spreadsheet.

What telemetry is still needed for is the business case's later gates, and that is where `SPEC3343` belongs.

**Decision at the end of the slice — written for four readable measures, not six:**

A pilot run today reads **T1, T3, T4 and T6**. T2 and T5 are hypotheses with no measurement source; they are
**not zeros, and not failures**, and they must never be entered into a decision as though a run produced them.

| Outcome | Decision |
|---|---|
| **T1 and T3 met, T4 met** | the product is usable and the pain recurs → proceed to the editor experience, then re-run with T5 once an offer and participant access are authorised |
| **T1 and T3 met, T4 not met** | usable but not recurring → the pain is one-off; do not build a commercial gate on it |
| **T3 met, T1 not met** | people will share a template but cannot start fast enough → fix the five-minute claim before anything else |
| **T1 or T3 not met** | the funnel or the first-run experience is wrong; no commercial work at all |
| **T6 not met** | the product is being carried by hand; do not scale the pilot until support cost drops |

**T2 and T5 keep their thresholds and gain a precondition instead of a result:**

- T2 becomes readable only when Site supplies a qualified-visitor/CTA denominator **and** this lane
  identifies an install/download source.
- T5 becomes readable only when a current offer and participant access are authorised.

Neither precondition is a schedule. Recording them this way keeps the hypothesis intact and prevents a
future reader from concluding the thresholds were abandoned, missed or scored zero.

## Named owner and next action

| Role | Owner | Next action |
|---|---|---|
| Slice owner | **tinytools** | ship the CLI as an installable artifact (`SPEC3343`), then run the pilot |
| Channel placement | **safe-revenue** | place the technical post and the landing-page link; they offered to coordinate |
| Pilot ledger | **tinytools** | the consented per-participant rows: start, first render, own template, repeat, price answer, support minutes |
| Offer under test | **site lane** | supply the current `PlanCatalogue` version so T5 is asked against a real offer |
| Decision | **workspace user** | read the four numbers (T1/T3/T4/T6) and choose an outcome; T2 and T5 are unread hypotheses, not inputs |

**The blocking dependency is not instrumentation.** It is consent and a ledger:

- **Consent** — the pilot records who agreed to be observed, and what exactly was recorded.
- **A ledger** — one row per participant per session: start, first successful render, whether the template was
  theirs, whether they returned unprompted, what they said about price, minutes of support. That is the whole
  instrument.
- **Site's offer** — T5 has to be asked against the *current* catalogue, so the Site's plan version is needed.

`SPEC3343` is not on the critical path for this slice. It becomes necessary when the cohort is large enough
that nobody can witness it, which is a post-pilot problem.

## What this slice deliberately does not do

- It does not build a pricing page, a landing page or a checkout: the site lane has all three.
- It does not add accounts here: identity is Graph's, per the business case.
- It does not promise revenue. It promises **information** — six numbers and a decision.

## Product Owner decision — 2026-11-28

Reviewed at checkpoint `6ab6cd2` and recorded in `product-owner/portfolio/agent-product-kit.md`:

> preserve all six SPEC3342 numeric thresholds. Measure T1/T3/T4/T5/T6 through the stated consented manual
> pilot records; T2 stays unmeasurable until Site gives a qualified-visitor/CTA denominator and Tinytools
> identifies install/download source. Do not substitute page views, infer installs, or treat
> `subscription.started` as stated WTP.

Accepted, and it changes what this slice can claim on the day it runs:

| Threshold | State after the decision |
|---|---|
| T1, T3, T4, T6 | measurable with the consented manual ledger |
| **T5** | **not measurable now** - no price ask is authorised and participant access is not approved; the threshold is preserved but cannot be read |
| **T2** | **not measurable now** - Site confirms no per-product visitor or purchase measure exists and its checkout is disabled; this lane has no install source |

So a pilot run today produces **four** readable numbers, not six. The other two stay in the table as
preserved hypotheses with their blocker named, which is more honest than deleting them or quietly
substituting something easier to count.

Three prohibitions the decision adds, all of which this slice already respected but now records explicitly:

1. **Do not substitute page views** for qualified visitors (T2 would become a ratio of two unrelated things).
2. **Do not infer installs** from opens (T1's start point would silently change meaning).
3. **Do not treat a subscription as stated willingness to pay** (T5 asks what a person said; a purchase is a
   different fact, and it is the Site's to supply).

What this decision does **not** authorise: telemetry, any threshold change, a consent mechanism, outreach,
a price ask, or participant access. All of those remain unapproved, so the slice cannot start on its own.

### Site's confirmed state, which is why T2 is blocked
