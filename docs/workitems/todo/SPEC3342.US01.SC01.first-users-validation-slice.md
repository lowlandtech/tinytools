## Why this exists

`safe-revenue` asked for the GA business case to be refined into a concrete validation slice: target user,
first-user channel, pilot ask, willingness-to-pay hypothesis and a measurable decision threshold with a named
owner. This is that slice, and it is deliberately small enough to run in weeks rather than quarters.

## The slice

> **A developer who already writes code-generation templates — Razor, T4, Scriban, or a home-grown
> string-concatenation script — installs the CLI, and sees one of their own templates render in under five
> minutes, then chooses whether to pay for the thing that saves them time afterwards.**

Everything below serves that sentence.

## Target user

Not "developers". The narrowest group that feels this product's pain today:

**A .NET developer maintaining generated code by hand** — DTOs, clients, migrations — who has already
reached for a template engine, which means they have already decided the problem is real. They are reachable
in places where that decision is visible: a T4/Razor question, a source-generator issue, a "how do you keep
your DTOs in sync" thread.

Excluded on purpose: people who have never generated code (they need the problem explained before the
product), and teams with a codegen platform already (they are an enterprise sale, not a first-user pilot).

## First-user channel

Ranked by cost per qualified visitor — a *qualified* visitor has already decided the problem is real:

| # | Channel | Why it fits | Cost | Measure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The CLI itself, published** | the fastest path from interest to "it worked": one command, one template, one result | 2 pts of packaging work | installs, first successful render |
| 2 | **A single technical post** on what a template engine with a language server actually gives you | reaches the person already looking; no audience needed if it lands in a community that has the question | 1 pt | reads → installs |
| 3 | **Direct outreach to 20 named developers** with a repo using T4 or Razor codegen | highest signal per person; slowest per hundred | 2 pts | replies, pilots started |
| 4 | The site lane's landing page and plan catalogue | it exists already | 0 in this lane | signups |

**Start at 1 and 2 together**, because they are the only two that can be live within a week and they
compose: the post is useless without something to install, and the CLI is invisible without a place to hear
about it.

## The pilot ask

Not a demo, not a survey. **One template, one session:**

> "Give me one template you maintain by hand. I will render it with TinyTools in front of you, and you keep
> the result. Ten minutes. If it does not save you time, say so and I will go away."

That ask is chosen because it produces a **behaviour**, not an opinion. Someone who says "interesting" has
told you nothing; someone who hands over a real template has revealed that the pain is real and specific.

## Willingness-to-pay hypothesis

**Hypothesis:** a developer who has rendered one of their own templates will pay for one of two things —
**not for the CLI** (developers expect CLIs free and there is no precedent for charging for one), but for
either:

- **the hosted collaboration surface**: templates and their rendered output shared with a team, with history
  — the thing that cannot be done in a shell; or
- **the editor experience**: the VSIX, once it actually verifies in an editor. Tooling that saves a keystroke
  budget is priced like an IDE add-on, not like a library.

**Price point to test:** two tiers against the site lane's existing `PlanCatalogue` — a free single-user tier
that is genuinely useful (so the CLI spreads), and one paid team tier. Do not invent a price; the catalogue
already expresses the tiers, and this slice tests whether a *subject* can be recognised as entitled, which
is the actual missing link measured in `SPEC3341`.

**The hypothesis I would put real money against being wrong:** that people will pay for the *library*. Open
source template engines are free and good. The product's paid value is collaboration and the editor, and if
that is not true, the commercial gate is not worth building.

## Measurable decision threshold

Fixed **before** the pilot runs, because a threshold chosen afterwards is a rationalisation:

| Metric | Threshold | What it decides |
|---|---|---|
| Time from install to first successful render | **< 5 minutes**, median | the product is usable enough to spread |
| Qualified visitors → installs | **≥ 10%** | the channel reaches the right people |
| Installs → a pilot session with a real template | **≥ 30%** | the ask works |
| Pilot sessions → "I would use this weekly" (behaviour: they render a second template unprompted) | **≥ 40%** | the pain is recurring, not one-off |
| Pilot sessions → willingness to pay at the catalogue price | **≥ 2 of 10** | a commercial gate is worth building now |
| Support burden per pilot | **< 30 minutes** | the product is not carried by hand-holding |

**Decision at the end of the slice:**

- **All six met** → build the commercial gate now, and the paid surface is collaboration + editor.
- **First three met, last three not** → the product is useful but not yet sellable; stay free, instrument,
  and re-run with the editor experience in the pilot.
- **First three not met** → the problem is the funnel or the five-minute claim, not the price. Fix that
  before spending anything on commerce.
- **Any one materially missed** → write down which and why, and do not add a tier to compensate.

## Named owner and next action

| Role | Owner | Next action |
|---|---|---|
| Slice owner | **tinytools** | ship the CLI as an installable artifact (`SPEC3343`), then run the pilot |
| Channel placement | **safe-revenue** | place the technical post and the landing-page link; they offered to coordinate |
| Instrumentation | **host lane (graph/harness)** | the event vocabulary from `SPEC3341` gate 2, without which none of the six metrics can be read |
| Commercial | **site lane** | the plans already exist; this slice only needs a subject to be recognisable as entitled |
| Decision | **workspace user** | read the six numbers and choose one of the four outcomes |

**The blocking dependency is instrumentation.** Five of the six thresholds are events. Without the gate-2
vocabulary this slice produces anecdotes, so it should not start until that exists — or the thresholds must
be reduced to what can be measured by hand, which weakens the result.

## What this slice deliberately does not do

- It does not build a pricing page, a landing page or a checkout: the site lane has all three.
- It does not add accounts here: identity is Graph's, per the business case.
- It does not promise revenue. It promises **information** — six numbers and a decision.
