## Why preparation is the correct next action

`safe-revenue` asked this lane to "identify and self-assign eligible evidence preparation for T1/T3/T4/T6".
The word *eligible* does the work: a pilot needs access and consent, which this lane does not have and cannot
grant itself. What it **can** do without either is make sure that the moment access exists, the measurement
is already defined.

That matters more than it sounds. The failure mode for a ten-person pilot is not a lack of data — it is
collecting nine numbers of the wrong shape and discovering afterwards that three of them cannot be compared.
A threshold whose row was designed after the fact is a threshold chosen to fit the result.

## The four decisions inside the instrument that matter

1. **`declined` and `withdrawn` are rows, not omissions.** Omitting them turns a refusal rate into silence
   and inflates every denominator. The instrument records a no.
2. **`not reached` counts as a T1 failure, not an exclusion.** A participant who never saw a render is the
   strongest possible signal against the five-minute claim; dropping them from the median hides exactly the
   outcome the threshold exists to detect.
3. **"Unprompted" is decided at the time, and ambiguity defaults to `no`.** Reconstructing it afterwards from
   memory is generous to the hypothesis, and the generous reading belongs to the participant.
4. **A purchase never substitutes for a stated answer.** T5's field takes one of four fixed categories; a
   subscription is a different fact, owned by the site lane.

## The instrument

# TinyTools first-user pilot — the measurement instrument

This is the instrument for `SPEC3342`'s four readable thresholds (T1, T3, T4, T6). It is **preparation, not
evidence**: it contains no participant, no observation and no number. Nothing here may be filled in until
participant access, outreach and consent are authorised, none of which is granted today.

The rule this document exists to enforce: **a threshold is only a threshold if the thing that produces its
number is written down before the number exists.**

## What may and may not be recorded

| May be recorded | Must never be recorded |
|---|---|
| participant alias, consent state, session dates | name, email, employer, contact details |
| that a template was the participant's own (yes/no) | template content, file paths, rendered output |
| elapsed minutes to first successful render | what the template says or does |
| whether a second template was rendered unprompted (yes/no) | the second template's content |
| minutes of support per session, category | quotes or notes containing identifiers |
| the price answer, as one of four fixed categories | a transcript, or anything the participant did not consent to |

Template content is excluded for the same reason `SPEC3343` excludes it from events: the instrument must be
safe to hand to an auditor. If an observation cannot be recorded without content, it is not recorded.

## Consent boundary

Before any row is created, the participant is told, in these terms and no vaguer:

> I would like to record six things about this session: the date, that you agreed, whether the template we
> render is one of yours, how long it took to see it work, whether you came back, and how many minutes of my
> time it took. I will not record your name, your templates, or anything they contain. You can withdraw at
> any time and I will delete the row.

The consent state is recorded as one of `given`, `declined`, `withdrawn`. **`declined` and `withdrawn` are
recorded, not omitted** — otherwise the denominator silently excludes people who said no, and a refusal rate
becomes invisible.

## The ledger

One row per participant per session. Blank template:

| Field | Values | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `participant` | alias, e.g. `p01` | pseudonymous; the mapping to a person is not stored |
| `consent` | `given` / `declined` / `withdrawn` | required before any other field |
| `session_date` | ISO date | |
| `t1_start_utc` | timestamp | the moment the participant begins installing |
| `t1_first_render_utc` | timestamp, or `not reached` | the moment they see their own template render |
| `t3_own_template` | `yes` / `no` / `not reached` | `yes` requires their attestation, not our assumption |
| `t4_returned_unprompted` | `yes` / `no` / `not reached` | defined below |
| `t4_second_template` | `yes` / `no` / `not reached` | |
| `t6_support_minutes` | integer, or `not recorded` | clock on and off, per session |
| `t6_support_category` | `install` / `template-authoring` / `concept` / `other` | |
| `notes` | free text **without identifiers or content** | empty is a valid value |

## How each threshold is derived

### T1 — time from install to first successful render, median < 5 minutes

`t1_first_render_utc - t1_start_utc`, in minutes, across participants where both fields are present.
Median, not mean: one participant who takes 40 minutes because they debug their own template should not move
the number that decides whether the first five minutes work.

Rows with `not reached` are **counted as failures of T1, not excluded** — a participant who never reaches a
render is the strongest possible signal about the five-minute claim. Report the `not reached` count beside
the median, always.

### T3 — installs reaching a real-template session, ≥ 30%

Numerator: rows where `t3_own_template = yes`. Denominator: rows where `consent = given`. A session that
never happened cannot be in either.

`yes` requires the participant to have confirmed the template is one they maintain. A template we supplied as
a demo is `no`, however well the session went.

### T4 — a second template unprompted, ≥ 40%

Two conditions, both required:

- `t4_returned_unprompted = yes` — they opened TinyTools again **without being reminded**; and
- `t4_second_template = yes` — they rendered a second template.

"Unprompted" is decided by the pilot runner at the time and written down as a yes/no; it is not reconstructed
afterwards from memory. If it is ambiguous, it is `no` — the generous reading belongs to the participant, not
to the metric.

### T6 — support burden per session, < 30 minutes

Sum of `t6_support_minutes` divided by the number of sessions with `consent = given`. Wall time spent on that
participant, by that participant, in that session — not time spent fixing a shared defect the session exposed
(those are logged separately as product work, not support).

## Traps this instrument exists to prevent

Each of these was a real risk in the decision that authorised this pilot, and each would produce a number
that looks like evidence and is not:

| Trap | Why it is wrong | Instrument rule |
|---|---|---|
| counting page views as qualified visitors | a view is not a person with the problem | T2 is not in this ledger at all |
| inferring installs from first launch | an install and a launch are different events, and neither is observed here | T1 starts from the participant's own start time |
| treating a subscription as stated willingness to pay | a purchase is a different fact from an answer | T5 has one field, a fixed category, and no purchase ever substitutes for it |
| dropping declining participants | turns a refusal rate into silence | `declined` and `withdrawn` are rows |
| reconstructing "unprompted" later | memory is generous to the hypothesis | decided and recorded at the time |
| excluding `not reached` from T1 | removes the worst outcomes from the average | counted as T1 failures, reported beside the median |

## What this document is not

It is not a pilot, an authorisation, a consent mechanism or evidence. It is the blank form plus the rules for
filling it. Running it requires, separately: participant access, outreach permission, a consent mechanism the
host lane owns, and Product Owner approval to begin — none of which is granted by this document or by
`SPEC3342`.
